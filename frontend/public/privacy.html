Satis Manager

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 November 2026

Satis Manager, the hosted Satisfactory Dash service, is a free dashboard for a Satisfactory dedicated server, paid for by ads. This page says what it collects, why, who else handles it, what the ads do, how long it's kept, and what you can ask for. It describes what the service actually does. It offers the same rights to everyone, wherever you live. For visitors in the European Economic Area (EEA), the UK and Switzerland it also gives the details their data protection law (the GDPR) asks for: who is responsible, the legal basis for each use, and where to complain. It doesn't claim any certification.

Who runs this

Satis Manager is run by an individual, Leonardo Nunez, who decides how its data is used. Contact: privacy@satis-manager.com.

In GDPR terms, the operator is the controller of the data this page describes, except the data Google collects for itself through the ads (see "Advertising").

What we don't do

  • No analytics of our own: we don't run scripts that measure how you use the site.
  • We don't sell the data we hold about you (your account, your servers, your game data), and we don't give it to anyone for marketing. Google's ad script collects data on our pages by itself, as "Advertising" below describes. Under some US state laws that collection can count as "selling" or "sharing" your personal information; "Your consent choices" below says how to opt out.
  • We don't track you across other sites ourselves. Google and its partners can, for personalised ads, if you allow them (see "Advertising"). We don't respond to a browser's "Do Not Track" signal; use the choices under "Your consent choices" instead.

Cookies and similar storage

  • Ours: only the ones the service needs to work. The session cookie keeps you signed in, and a short-lived cookie (10 minutes) protects the Google sign-in. They need no consent.
  • Google's, for the ads: Google and its partners may place and read cookies in your browser, and use your IP address, web beacons, local storage and similar technologies, to show ads, limit how often you see the same one, count and report them, and detect fraud. Google sets its cookies from its own domains, such as google.com, doubleclick.net and googlesyndication.com. Which of these are used depends on your consent choice, below.
  • Your consent choice itself is stored in your browser by Google's consent message, so it isn't asked again on every page.

Advertising

Satis Manager shows ads from Google AdSense. They pay for running the service.

Where ads appear

  • On the public pages: the home page, and guides and the changelog once they exist.
  • In the app, in a few fixed places beside the content, such as below a page's content or in a side area. Never over the factory graph, the map or a table of game data, and never in a pop-up.
  • Never on the sign-in, error, pairing, account or settings screens, and never on this page or the Terms of Use.
  • The demo site (demo.satis-manager.com) shows no ads.

What Google's ad script can see

To show ads, a script from Google runs in the page itself, on the public pages and in the app. Like any script in a page, it can read what the page shows. In the app that includes your servers' names, your factories, the names players give things in the game, the names of players who are online, and anything else on your screen. We don't send Google your account details, but we can't stop its script from reading the page.

Your browser also sends Google the address of the page you're on and your IP address. Google may use the IP address to work out your general location, to measure how well ads work and, depending on your settings, to pick more relevant ads. Google keeps a record of the ads it serves, with your IP address, browser details and cookies; it says it removes part of the IP address after 9 months and the cookie information after 18 months.

Google decides for itself how it uses what it collects through its ads. Under its advertising data protection terms, it is an independent controller of that data, not someone acting on our behalf. How Google uses that data is explained in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services, and in Google's advertising page.

Personalised, non-personalised and limited ads

  • Personalised ads: Google and its partners use cookies to pick ads based on your visits to this site and other sites on the internet. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, only with your consent.
  • Non-personalised ads: picked from the page's topic and your general location, not from a profile of you. They still use cookies to limit how often you see the same ad and to report on ads in total, so in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland they need your consent to cookies too.
  • Limited ads: shown when you haven't consented, including before you've made a choice. Google's script loads before you choose, so a limited ad can appear while the consent message is still open. Limited ads use no personal data to personalise ads, but your IP address is still used to deliver the ad, and Google uses cookies, local storage and the browser's Shared Storage, only to detect invalid traffic and fraud. Google doesn't ask for consent for this.

Other ad companies that Google works with may also place and read cookies to show ads here. You can opt out of their use of cookies for personalised advertising on the industry pages below.

Opting out of personalised ads

  • Google: turn off ad personalisation in Google's Ad Settings. This works on every site that uses Google's ads, not just this one.
  • Other ad companies: aboutads.info (US-based) and Your Online Choices (EU-based).
  • Your browser: most browsers can block third-party cookies or clear the ones they hold. Google notes that even then it may use other advertising technologies to personalise ads unless you turn personalisation off in Ad Settings.

Your consent choices

If you visit from the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, Google's consent message asks you first whether Google and its partners may use cookies and your data for ads. You can consent, decline, or pick what to allow. Declining doesn't stop you using any part of the service; you'll see limited ads instead.

You can change or withdraw your choice at any time with the "Privacy and cookie settings" link in the footer of the home page and of the app. It reopens the consent message with all three options: consent, decline, or manage options. Withdrawing doesn't affect what was done with your consent before. This page loads no scripts, so the link isn't here.

If you're in a US state with its own privacy law (such as California), a "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link in the footer of the home page and the app lets you opt out of the sale or sharing of your personal information for ads.

Google's ad crawler

Google needs to see the pages its ads appear on, including in the app. So its ad crawler gets its own account: a viewer on one server that holds only demo data. It can't see any real user's server, factory or game data. Its password is kept in Google's AdSense settings and in our secret store, never in the source code.

Who else handles data

  • Google: signing in. It tells us your Google account id (and your email address, which we don't keep for accounts created by signing up).
  • Google, again: the ads (Google AdSense) and the consent message. Its script runs in our pages and collects data for itself, as an independent controller, as described under "Advertising". Other ad companies Google works with may receive data through it too.
  • Cloudflare: hosting, DNS, the network tunnel and the firewall. It sees every request's IP address and metadata.
  • Backblaze: stores our encrypted backups.
  • Amazon Web Services: hosts our app server, its database and the server logs (kept 14 days). It also still holds our older encrypted backups. Nothing new is added to them, and they're removed within 37 days.
  • Discord: only if a server's owner or an admin sets up a webhook. It receives that server's alert messages (the server's name and what the alert is about: a circuit, or a count of machines and their recipes). No account information is sent.   • Your browser's push service (Google, Mozilla or Microsoft, depending on your browser): only if you turn on push notifications. It carries alert messages to your device. Messages are encrypted end to end, so it sees only that a message was sent, when, and how urgent it is. We keep the subscription, not the messages.

When a new feature needs another provider (for example, emailed alerts), it's added here before that feature goes live.

These providers are based in the United States, so your data is processed there.

For visitors in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, that is a transfer of personal data outside Europe. Each of these providers offers its own legal safeguard for such transfers (for example an adequacy decision or the European Commission's standard contractual clauses), and you can ask us which one applies to a provider.

Abuse, and removing servers

Removing a server only removes it from Satisfactory Dash. It never touches your world or your save files. Those live on your own machine, and the service has no way to change or delete them. Your game server keeps running exactly as before. Its agent is signed out, and the dashboard stops receiving its data.

If someone abuses the service, the operator can disable their account and remove their servers from Satisfactory Dash. Disabling signs the person out, stops Google sign-in for that account, and signs out the programs connecting the servers it owns. Their server data is kept unless a server is also removed.

When a server is removed, by its owner or by the operator, we delete what we stored for it:

  • its name
  • the people who belong to it, and their roles
  • the encrypted game-server tokens
  • the history of power, production and machine states
  • alert rules, alert history, mutes, and the encrypted Discord webhook
  • push-notification sign-ups for that server
  • the saved map layers
  • setup codes, the agent credential, and any pending commands

We keep a short record that the server existed: its id, when it was created and, for a server someone added themselves, the user id of the account that added it (until that account is deleted). We also keep the audit events about it. None of these hold emails or names of people. Backups can still hold the deleted data until they age out, within 37 days.

A server that is added but never connected is removed the same way once its waiting time runs out (currently 24 hours).

How long it's kept

  • Your account: until you delete it.
  • Sessions: session records are deleted 30 days after the session expires (a session lasts at most 8 hours).
  • Game data (power, production and machine states): recorded so the dashboard can show history and send alerts. Detailed readings are kept for 48 hours, one-minute averages for 7 days, and hourly averages for 1 year. Machine state changes are kept for 30 days. It describes the game world, not you. Player names and player counts are never part of it.
  • The latest reading of each server (status, power and factory; never player names): one copy, replaced as new readings arrive, deleted when the server is removed.
  • Alerts (which alert fired or cleared, and when): kept for 90 days. A message that could not be delivered to Discord is tried again for up to 24 hours and then given up.
  • Backups: Backblaze stores encrypted backups for up to 37 days, so deleted data can survive in backups for up to 37 days. The older copies Amazon Web Services still holds are removed within 37 days too.
  • Data for a server removed from Satisfactory Dash: deleted at once (the list above), except in backups, which age out within 37 days.
  • Audit events: 1 year. They're kept for security and hold no emails or names. They hold a pseudonymous user id, and that stays for the year even after you delete your account.
  • Server logs: 14 days. A request's log line holds no IP address. Your IP address is logged only on sign-in events and when a rate limit or abuse protection steps in.
  • System metrics (counts and timings of requests and background jobs, with no personal data beyond server ids): 90 days.
  • Commands sent to a server's agent: 7 days after they finish.
  • Pairing requests (the server's session name and agent version): deleted about an hour after they expire.
  • Push-notification subscriptions: until you remove them, or your browser's push service drops them.
  • Data Google collects through the ads: Google keeps it under its own policies (see "Advertising"); we don't hold a copy.

Legal bases

Under the GDPR, every use of personal data needs a legal basis. These are ours:

  • To run the service you asked for (each table row marked so, above): performance of our contract with you, the Terms of Use.
  • To keep the service safe (security, rate limiting, sign-in security logs, the operator's server lists, and the audit events, kept for a year even after an account is deleted): our legitimate interest in protecting the service and its users from abuse.
  • Personalised ads, and the cookies and similar storage that non-personalised ads use: your consent, through Google's consent message. You can withdraw it at any time (see "Your consent choices").
  • Limited ads' invalid-traffic cookies and local storage, used before or without consent: the legitimate interest in detecting fraud and invalid traffic on the ads that pay for the service. You can object to it, as below.
  • Showing ads at all, on pages where Google's script runs: our legitimate interest in paying for a free service. What Google itself does with the data rests on Google's own legal bases, explained in its privacy policy.

Your choices and rights

Whoever and wherever you are, you can ask to see the data we hold about you, get a copy of it, correct it, or delete it, and you can object to how it's used. Email privacy@satis-manager.com. We'll check that the request comes from the account's owner before acting on it, and we'll answer within 30 days.

Where we rely on your consent, you can withdraw it at any time, as easily as you gave it (see "Your consent choices"). For the data Google collects through the ads, you can also use Google's own controls, or contact Google directly.

If you're in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland and think we've handled your data wrongly, you can complain to a data protection authority, for example the one where you live or work (in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office). We'd like the chance to put it right first, so please email us too.

You can delete your account yourself, in Settings, under "Delete my account". It asks for a sign-in from the last 10 minutes first, so nobody can do it from a computer you left signed in. In Settings you can also leave a server you belong to but don't own. Or email us, as above.

Deleting your account removes your user record, your sign-in identity, your sessions, your memberships and your push-notification subscriptions, at once. Servers you own are removed from Satisfactory Dash with it, as described in "Abuse, and removing servers" (if you own a server the operator added, that one is handed over or removed first). Servers you only belong to are left as they are. Audit events about your actions are kept for their year; they hold your user id, not your email or name. You can also stop using Google sign-in for us at any time from your Google account settings.

Security

User accounts sign in with Google, so we don't store passwords for them. Session ids are stored hashed; the database gives each part of the service only the access it needs; the site runs under a Content Security Policy; and the API can only be reached through Cloudflare. No system is perfectly secure, but these are the efforts we make. If a breach affects your data, we'll tell you without undue delay.

One trade-off to know about: to show ads, the pages that carry them let Google's script run and load further scripts, so their Content Security Policy is looser than it would be without ads. This page and the Terms of Use load no scripts at all.

Children

Satis Manager is for adults: you must be at least 18 to use it. If we learn an account belongs to someone under 18, we delete it.

Changes to this policy

The date at the top changes with every update. For a significant change, we'll also put a notice on the site before it takes effect.
